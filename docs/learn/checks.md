# Checks and limits

A check records its method and input scope. Four core examples here have separate records. The law records 64 checked input pairs and the bridge 16 checked inputs; their badges describe those bounded scopes. The hidden-card exercise is a design. In the exported walkthrough, `@twice[bump_even]` on <code>Even.val&lt;20&gt;</code> returns <code>Even.val&lt;24&gt;</code>. The exporter passes that result as the prior observation input to `observe` through a granted `read_number` probe; the observation does not call the combinator.

<p class="a2-learn-lede">Seeing a signal is not seeing the hidden state. A certificate is the evidence record for a specific definition and scope, not for its name alone.</p>

The three comparison cards describe methods, not completed checks. Open a Designed example badge to inspect what is recorded and what is not. A method description is not a certificate.

## World export limitations

World exports include historical recorded cases and design declarations. A design declaration may describe behavior in prose rather than use current-core ASCII syntax. Its parser count measures source compatibility, not whether the recorded-case tower works. The browser does not rerun those cases through the current core.
